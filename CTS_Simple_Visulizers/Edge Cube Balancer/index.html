Where I'd push back

  • The motor is small for a cube. The 2804 gives about 0.03 N·m. A cube's centre of mass sits 0.71 of its side above the pivot edge, so at 90 to 120 mm gravity pulls with 0.10 to 0.16 N·m per radian of lean. The motor can only out-pull gravity up to about 17 degrees at 90 mm and 11 degrees at 120 mm, and the angle it can actually recover from is smaller than that.
  • There is a size that works, and it is narrow. From 90 mm up, torque is the limit and the recoverable lean keeps falling as the cube grows. At 80 mm and below the wheel runs out of speed first, so a smaller cube stops helping much. The bar colours in the chart show which limit applies.
  • The wheel axis has to lie along the pivot edge. In the first sketch the wheel faced out of the plate, across the edge. Tick the box to see it: the motor spins up and the cube falls anyway.
  • A flat plate with the wheel low would balance better. In the same simulation, with the same motor, a 100 x 150 mm plate with the wheel 45 mm up recovers about 20 degrees, against about 13 for a 90 mm cube, because its centre of mass is lower. The cube is better as a complete package, not as an easier balance.
  • A cube on its corner is a different project. Three axes need three motors and a much bigger torque budget than 0.03 N·m. Treat this as step one, and keep the frame able to take two more wheels.
  • It cannot jump up. Standing a Cubli up from rest needs a brake that dumps wheel momentum in a hurry. Here you set it on its edge by hand and let go inside the recoverable angle.
  • A few millimetres of imbalance matters. At 90 mm the centre of mass is 64 mm up, so 2 mm sideways moves the balance point by about 1.8 degrees. Plan trim weights and let the controller learn the offset.
  • The numbers are the ideal case. Perfect sensors, no delay, no friction, no noise. Expect less on the bench.

What the cube gets right

  • Nothing trails behind it. The battery and electronics ride inside, so no cable tugs on the pivot, which is the biggest disturbance the free-standing plate had.
  • The frame is the pivot. One edge is the contact line, so there is no hinge or clip to build.
  • The centre of mass is symmetric and the wheel is protected by the frame when it falls.
  • It grows. The same frame can take a second and third wheel later, and a bigger motor such as the 4015 kit in the same Amazon listing, which its seller rates at 0.32 N·m (an unchecked claim).

Model: the cube pivots about its bottom edge; the motor's torque on the wheel pushes back on the frame; a three-state controller (lean, lean rate, wheel speed) with poles placed at 1.5, 1.9 and 0.6 times the cube's own falling rate; torque capped at the limit and reduced as the wheel nears its 272 rad/s no-load speed at 12 V. The cube's frame is 12 printed bars 5 mm square at 80 % of solid PLA; the wheel scales from your rev3 D100 wheel.
