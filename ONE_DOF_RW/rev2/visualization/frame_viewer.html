Design notes

  1. Slide-in walls. 3.0 mm panels in 3.6 mm pillar slots (0.3 mm clearance per side for PLA), seated in a 2 mm baseplate channel. Panels stop 17 mm under the pillar tops, below the lock rods and the lid skirts, so nothing on the lid has to reach down beside them. A side panel can lift about 7 mm before it hits a rod. Screw boards on before a panel goes in.
  2. One wall for all electronics. The 90 × 70 perf board (ESP32 + IMU) and the red 43 × 43 L298N module sit side by side on the left wall, 133 of the 140 mm between pillars, with the motor plugs facing them. The L298N drives the motor and its 78M05 makes the ESP32's 5 V, so the MD13S and any buck module are gone. That makes the old back wall and the four trusses unnecessary.
  3. L298N limits. It drops about 2 V across its bridge, so a full 12.6 V pack puts about 10 V on a 6 V TT motor. Keep the firmware's 50% duty cap. The 78M05 sees 12.6 V, just past the 12 V jumper rating, and burns about 1 W feeding the ESP32, so check it for heat or move the ESP32 to a small 5 V buck. The firmware needs ENA = PWM and IN1/IN2 for direction instead of the MD13S's PWM/DIR.
  4. One-piece lid. A 168 × 168 flat X on the four pillar tops, printed as one part, with a printed eye at the centre. Each corner block wraps 15 mm of its pillar top. It replaces 4 arms, 4 caps and a hub (9 parts). Arms are 8 × 10, which keeps sag under about 0.3 mm with the whole platform hanging from the middle.
  5. Printed rods, pillar to pillar. A 5 mm printed rod runs through both lid blocks and both pillars above the left wall, and another above the right. Holes are 5.4 mm. Print each rod lying flat so the layers run along it, with a head on one end and a printed cotter through the other; print a few spares. Working in shear, the rods carry the platform's weight, which a vertical friction pin would not. Back and front panels, if fitted, are held by the lid covering their slot tops.
  6. Balance with the pack. About 55 g of boards on the left wall is cancelled by the 150 g pack under the plate, sitting about 25 mm toward +x, so the steel trim weight can drop to zero. The tray's four slotted holes let it slide 30 mm in x and 1.5 mm in y, and the trim weight on the right wall only appears if the pack runs out of travel.
  7. Hook is close to the CoM. Tilt is the CoM offset divided by the hook-to-CoM distance, now about 80 mm instead of about 180 mm. Each millimetre of offset costs about 0.7°. A taller eye post lowers that.
  8. IMU +Y along the string. The firmware reads the gyro's Y axis, so the GY-521 must sit with +Y vertical. Tie a single strand of monofilament or thin steel wire to the eye; twisted twine unwinds and creeps.
